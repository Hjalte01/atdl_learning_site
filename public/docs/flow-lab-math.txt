RECTIFIED FLOW LAB — AN ANALYTIC TEACHING EXAMPLE

This is not a trained FLUX model or a measurement of its latent space.
Convention follows the course paper, section 3: z_t = (1-t)x + t epsilon.

Target distribution: an equal mixture of three isotropic 2D Gaussians.
Centers mu_k = (-1.15,-0.75), (1.1,-0.6), (0,1.2).
Each component has covariance s_x^2 I, where s_x^2 = 0.075.
Noise epsilon ~ N(0,I), independent of x.

Let a = 1-t. Given component k:
  E[z_t|k] = a mu_k
  Var(z_t|k) = S I, S = a^2 s_x^2 + t^2
  u = epsilon-x
  E[u|k] = -mu_k
  Cov(u,z_t|k) = (t-a s_x^2) I

Gaussian conditioning therefore gives:
  E[u|z_t=z,k] = -mu_k + ((t-a s_x^2)/S)(z-a mu_k)

Posterior component probabilities w_k are proportional to:
  exp(-||z-a mu_k||^2 / (2S))
The priors and component determinants are equal and cancel.

The exact squared-error-optimal field is:
  v(z,t) = sum_k w_k E[u|z_t=z,k]

The simulation uses explicit Euler integration BACKWARD IN TIME:
  z_next = z - (1/N) v(z,t)
  t_next = t - 1/N
This is not the implicit numerical method called "backward Euler".

The vector arrows display normalized -v (generation direction), not velocity
magnitude. The same reproducible Gaussian starting sample is used when changing
N. Target outlines are illustrative level regions, not hard boundaries.
Finite step counts introduce numerical error. At t=0 and t=1 the variances stay
positive. There is no singular endpoint in this chosen toy distribution.

The two-arrow experiment is separate: two equally weighted pairs intersect at
z=(3,2), t=0.5 with forward velocities (4,-2) and (4,2), averaging to (4,0).
It illustrates conditional-mean regression locally, not a complete sampler.
